## 3. Technical Learnings

- ⚠️ **Four reversals this session; only ONE was caught by the run itself.**
  Two by Peter, one by Fable. **Every one was a CONFIDENT NEGATIVE** — "nothing
  reads this", "this can never fire", "the search couldn't have found it". Not
  one was a wrong positive. **That claim shape is now named in the skill.**
- ⚠️ **A grep over `DECISIONS.md` is not a check that something is settled** —
  but the real failure was simpler: **the grounding step was skipped, and the
  recovery was truncated by `cut`.** Beware turning an omission into a lesson.
- ⚠️ **Writing the bias warning is not executing it.** `FINDINGS_guard_channels.md`
  opens with a self-audit caveat, then publishes the bias anyway. Kept in place
  deliberately as the most useful line in the file.
- **A cross-model audit relocates measurement bias; it does not remove it.**
  S164 (Fable) and S165 (Opus) each over-claimed in opposite directions on the
  same guard. What caught both was **re-measurement**, not the model swap.
- **`git push` RECREATES a deleted remote branch.** Auto-delete-on-merge is not
  stranding protection.
- **`git` IS the doc-growth time series** — `git ls-tree -r -l <rev>` at two
  revs gives per-file mass. Nothing derives it; that's the whole gap.
- ⚠️ **Read logs are blind to `bash` reads.** `retrieval_report.py`'s hook sees
  `Read`/`Grep`/`Glob` only; this session consulted ~14 docs via `sed`/`grep`
  and logged a handful. A zero there is not evidence of "never read".
