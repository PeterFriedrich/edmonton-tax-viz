#### A. The defect

Three WESTMOUNT **commercial storefronts** (12203/12207/12211 107 Avenue NW,
accounts 4259396/4259412/4259420, $859,500 combined) carry `lot_size`
0.505 / 0.218 / 0.277 — **normalized ownership shares summing to exactly
1.000**, a unit error, not a small lot. $859,500 ÷ 1.0 m² =
**$3,478,273,095/lot-acre**, matching the served file to the dollar.

⚠️ **The damage was not the wrong number — it was that the wrong number set the
SCALE.** `gridScale` (`web/index.html`) anchors spike elevation on
`vals[vals.length - 1]`, the **maximum** cell, so the tallest cell is drawn at
full prism height *by construction*. Colour clamps at p97.5; **height does
not**. One artifact flattened all 93,201 cells:

| column | × p97.5 | p97.5 rendered at |
|---|---|---|
| `value_per_lot_acre` | 402× | **0.249%** of full height |
| `revenue_per_lot_acre` | 937× | **0.107%** of full height |

Both `grid-fine` and `Lot acres` are **ungated**, so this was on the public root.

#### B. The fix, and why it has this shape

`MULTI_UNIT_MIN_LOT_M2 = 10.0` in `src/export_value_grid.py`: a point holding
**multiple** titled records whose total deduped lot area is under 10 m² is not
on a real parcel, so it leaves the lot-acre metric. Ground acres untouched —
the dollars are real, only the lot area is not. It lands in `_point_lot_stats`,
the single choke point that the grid, `build_hood_lot_acres`,
`check_lot_acre_bounds` **and** `check_value_anchors.py` all call.

⚠️ **Two wrong rules were measured and rejected before this one.** Recording
them because both looked obviously right:

1. **Record-level floor at `lot_size < 1 m²`** — cascades 66 points into
   `majority_null` and drops **$1.43B (0.598% of city value)** to remove
   $859,500. Sub-1 m² values are a **continuous graded series**
   (0.279/0.558/0.837/1.394/5.02 — multiples of one base share) inside
   400-unit towers, so a 1 m² cut halves a coherent population.
2. **Point-level floor at 10 m² without the `n > 1` restriction** — catches 381
   single-record points holding **real** tiny parcels (median 8.4 m², median
   value **$500** — stalls, slivers) to catch 1 defect.

The shipped rule is **insensitive from 2–50 m²** (one point throughout): it
sits in an empty gap, so the threshold is not a tuning knob.

## 3. Technical Learnings

### ⚠️ THE SESSION'S THEME — Peter's *"isn't option a less honest"* was right, and it reversed my recommendation

I proposed two fixes and recommended **both, A first**:
- **A** — clamp spike height at a percentile instead of anchoring on the max.
- **B** — fix the `lot_size` denominator.

Peter pushed back on A. He was right, and more strongly than he framed it:

⚠️ **The codebase had already decided it.** `web/index.html`: *"The sqrt is a
runtime choice via the Colour Adjustment toggle… off => linear+clamp (true
magnitude). **Height stays LINEAR regardless.**"* Colour is the *legible*
channel — it clamps, it can be sqrt-transformed. **Height is the truthful
channel, deliberately left linear.** A clamps the one channel the project
explicitly protects.

⚠️ **And the renderer was not the thing lying.** Every cell's height was a
correct linear proportion of the max. The map faithfully said "one thing here
is 400× everything else" — a false statement because the *data* was false.
**A would have made the map look fine while still containing the false
number.** Worse than the disease.

**Transferable rule: when a display looks wrong, establish whether the
rendering is lying or faithfully drawing a lie it was given.** My "generic
fragility" argument for A was also weaker than I presented — after B, p97.5
still renders at 1.414%, but that compression is **true**: Downtown genuinely
is 70× the p97.5 cell, and a map whose thesis is that downtown is wildly more
productive per acre should show that range.

### ⚠️ TWO stale "it can't be seen" claims were protecting the same defect

Both reasoned about **picking and colour** and both missed **height**:

1. `TODO.md`: *"Not user-visible — the Glass grid is `gridPickable: false` and
   the legend clamps at p97.5."*
2. `scripts/check_value_anchors.py`'s own docstring: *"It never reaches the map
   … so pinning it would have failed the build over an artifact nobody can
   see."* — written when 100 m was the only grid, **and the guard reads the
   very file the map now serves.**

⚠️ **A claim that something is invisible must enumerate every channel it can be
seen through.** Two of three were checked; the unchecked one was the one that
mattered. This is the `check-the-affordance-you-remove` / `guards-must-measure-
data-not-metadata` family.

### ⚠️ The old needle band could never have caught this

`lot_needle_ratio`'s band was **39.5–118.52**, fitted *around* the needle on
2026-09-01, so a reading of **79** sat comfortably inside it. The guard was
green the whole time the defect was live. **Re-pinned to 6.96–20.89 (reading
13.93) — the anchor detects a new needle for the first time.**

⚠️ **A band fitted around a defect is a band that cannot detect it.** Same
family as "an assertion placed where the value can't be wrong passes under the
bug."

### ⚠️ `ineligible_points` was at its band ceiling EXACTLY — unrelated to this work

Reading **84**, band max **84.0**. It would have red the next weekly publish on
its own. Only **+1** of the move to 85 is the new rule; the rest is the organic
drift `_ineligible_pair_is_drifting` predicted (56→58→60→84). Re-pinned to
42.5–127.5 at the same ±50%. ⚠️ **`ineligible_value_frac` has now consumed 83%
of its band** (its own note estimated 72%) — still trending, still the open
cause.

### ⚠️ A merged PR whose only served change is DATA deploys NOTHING

Found answering *"does the data need to be rerun."* `deploy.yml` triggers on
`web/**` **minus `!web/data/**`**, plus `scripts/build_site.py` and itself. #312
changed ten files — `src/export_value_grid.py`, three `web/data/*`, tests, docs
— and **zero matched**. Merge green, **no deploy run at all**, build stamp

**#312 shipped only because the refresh was dispatched by hand.** Now in

adding `src/**` to the deploy paths** — deploy regenerates nothing, and the
`!web/data/**` exclusion stops a code deploy racing the refresh's own deploy on
the shared `refresh-map-data` concurrency group.

**Where the build number lives** (Peter asked): the **Data & Methods** panel
(`#about-btn`, bottom right) → `<p id="about-build">`, filled from the
`BUILD_STAMP` literal. Format `<sha7> · <UTC date>`; `dev` means a local build.
It stamps the **code**, not the data — `#about-vintage` above it covers data.

### A false alarm worth recording, because the negative result is reusable

I found **23% of cells claim more lot acres than the cell physically contains**
(up to 585×) and thought I had a second defect. **I did not.** The #2 Downtown
cell holds two parcels, one of 3,754 m² whose centroid lands in a 2,500 m²
cell — parcels larger than cells are fully attributed to the cell containing
their point. **Benign geometry, expected.** I also checked the
share-multiplication branch (`lot_size < SHARE_MAX_M2` counted per unit): the
biggest cases are 147-unit townhouse complexes at 230 m² each = 8.4 acres,
plausible for individually titled rowhouse lots. **The top cells other than
Westmount are sound.**
