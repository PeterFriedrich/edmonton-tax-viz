## 3. Technical Learnings

### ⚠️ THE SESSION'S THEME — the project had already disproved its own cross-check and never joined the halves

`data/city_unit_costs.json` → `roadway_om_renewal.cross_check` claimed the City's
~3%/yr set-aside rule (3% of $1.5M ≈ **$45,000/km/yr**) *"corroborates $50"*.
**It cannot.** The City defines that 3% **on the same page** as covering
*operate + maintain + renew + replace* — the same $600k + $1.9M bundle over the
same life. $45k vs $50k is a rounded 3% against the bundle's actual **3.33%/yr**.

⚠️ **And this project had ALREADY COMPUTED that 3.33%** — in
`bikeway_capital.three_percent_rule_does_not_transfer`, written 2026-08-04, in
the same file, ~90 lines below. It held both halves of its own refutation and
never connected them.

⚠️ **What survives, and this matters — do not over-correct.** The rule still
*discriminates* 50-yr from 25-yr **directionally** (3.33% vs 6.67%), because the
life is the only free variable. That much was always legitimate. What was not is
calling it *independent*. **The $50 value is unchanged and the 50-year choice is
NOT reopened** — that was Peter's 2026-07-15 call on the page's own *"extended to
50 with proper maintenance"*, never on the 3% rule.

Propagated to **three** places, all corrected: the JSON, `TODO.md`'s open
service-life item (which leaned on it as *"why 50 still looks right"*), and the
S114 `AUDIT_LEDGER` row.

**Transferable rule: a cross-check built from the same inputs as the thing it
checks cannot fail.** Same family as *an assertion placed where the value can't
be wrong* (S133) and *a band fitted around a defect* (S133).

### ✅ Table 8 — the split §19 said was unpublished

`DATA.md` §19's *"NEW-VS-RENEWAL IS NOT A PUBLISHED FIELD"* is true of the CSV
but steers a reader into the `DATA_INTEGRITY.md` T8 hand-enumeration the same
bullet warns against. **Table 8 "Adopted Capital Budget by Service", pp.56–57**
of the December 2022 budget book carries explicit Growth/Renewal columns per
service ($M, 2023–26, **verified verbatim against the PDF**):

| line | Growth | Renewal | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| Active Pathways and Roads Service | 622.8 | 1,171.2 | 1,794.0 |
| Yellowhead Trail Freeway Conversion | 493.4 | — | 493.4 |
| **Grand Total** | 4,997.6 | 2,194.9 | **7,192.5** |

⚠️ **Three methods agree on renewal (~15% spread) and disagree on new by ~1.4×** —
keyword pass ~$1.49B vs Table 8 $1.12B, because the keyword pass sweeps in ~$326M
of active-transport, pedway and streetscape profiles. §19's *"do not publish a
split sourced that way"* is vindicated; Table 8 is the way out.

⚠️ **The trap: Table 8 and the CSV are different VINTAGES of the same cycle** —
as adopted (**$7,192.5M**) vs as since adjusted (**$9,216.8M, +28%**). Applying
Table 8's *ratio* to the CSV's *total* silently blends them.

### ⚠️ A composite profile does not carry its program's spend

`CM-25-0000 Transportation: Neighbourhoods - Renewal` reads **$69.8M** against the
December 2022 book's **$545.7M** for the same profile — decomposed into **45**
standalone named profiles (Ottewell $89.8M, Dunluce $89.6M, McCauley $64.5M,
Baturyn $45.0M…) filed under the **`Neighbourhoods` service, not `Roads`**.
**~$475M undercount.**

⚠️ **Nastier than the keyword trap already in §19, because it punishes doing the
obvious right thing** — reaching for the one profile the City names after the
program. Verified against the **committed** CSV, not the /tmp fetch.
