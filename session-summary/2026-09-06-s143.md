## 3. Technical Learnings

### The audit's finding (why the column went)

`svc_cost_per_acre` = roads lifecycle + `fire_events_per_acre × (Fire Rescue
budget ÷ citywide kept events)`. Measured from the **served** geojson:

- **88.6% of its variance was the fire term** (roads 3.4%). Spearman **0.956**
  with dispatches vs **0.776** with road metres. A column labelled *cost*
  coloured land by fire-dispatch density in dollars — under a caveat stating the
  budget does not move with dispatches.
- It assigned **MCCAULEY $57,990/acre/yr on 370 acres = $20.8M/yr, 7.5% of the
  entire Fire Rescue budget**, and read it at **0.5×** coverage. MCCAULEY and
  ELMWOOD PARK (118%) were the only two *developed* hoods under 1×; the other 20
  are set-aside river-valley/Henday strips.
- The four hoods topping it (MCCAULEY, DOWNTOWN, BOYLE STREET, CENTRAL
  MCDOUGALL) take **19.9% of all kept dispatches** and **22.9% of the medical
  ones**; their mixes are **63–78% medical** (citywide **60.6%**, up from the
  ~57% the docs record). 48 of 408 hoods generate half of all dispatches.
- ⚠️ **The killer argument:** the 2026-08-03 rule that denied
  `transport_cost_ops_per_acre` a UI row — *"a map coloured by the sum is a
  bus-service density map"*, 97.6% transit variance — **was never applied to
  this composite**, which had three surfaces. Same defect, opposite treatment,
  one month apart.
- **Transit survives on a real distinction:** it allocates a *supplied* service
  by supply (ETS chooses the schedule; costs scale with service-hours), where
  fire allocated *standing capacity* by demand. Recorded in
  `city_unit_costs.json` → `transit_ets.why_this_allocation_survived_the_audit`
  so a fourth allocation gets tested against it.

### ⚠️ The three traps in the removal — all real, all silent

1. **`hoodPanelLens()` was gated on `state.hasSvcCost`**, a flag computed from
   the *data*, not the build. The **public** build carried the column in its
   geojson and never displayed it, so the obvious removal would have **silently
   killed the entire Services hood panel on the public site**, roads rows
   included. Now gates on `cost_roads_life_per_acre`.
2. **`check_served_columns.py` FAILs (exit 5) on a removed baselined column** —
   `data/expected_columns.json` had to be re-pinned in the same PR or the next
   Sunday refresh would have gone red. While there, `cost_roads_life_per_acre`
   was **also pinned**: it had never been (nor has `rev_frac_exempt`, still
   unpinned — a candidate, not done).
3. **`load_unit_costs` hard-required `fire_response`.** Now optional; the JSON
   block stays as the fire lens's sourcing record with a `_status` saying
   nothing reads it.

### ⚠️ TWO MORE CHECKS THAT COULD NOT FAIL — this is the session's real finding

Both had been green for a month and both are now in the memory
`check-where-the-value-can-be-wrong` (updated, not duplicated):

- **`verify-transport-cost.js` check (3)**, "the two road bases stay distinct",
  compared `cost_roads_ops_per_acre` against **`svc_cost_per_acre`** and called
  it "life". The **fire term supplied the entire gap** — one rate wired into
  both road columns would have passed it. Now compares the two road columns and
  pins the **10.79×** ratio the rates imply.
- **`tests/test_join_and_calculate.py`'s `_TRANSPORT_COSTS` had BOTH road rates
  at `$2.0`** (`road_dollars_per_m` and `road_ops_dollars_per_m`), so
  `test_transport_ops_roads_term_differs_from_lifecycle_roads_term` was vacuous
  in the fixture as well as in the comparator. Now 20.0 vs 2.0, mirroring the
  real 10.8×, and the test pins the *gap*, not the two values.

**New sub-shape worth carrying:** when the check is a **comparison**, confirm
both sides contain only what the claim names — an extra term on either side
manufactures the difference. And check the **fixture's constants**, not just the
assertion.

### ⚠️ A PUBLIC-BUILD CONTENT LEAK, pre-existing, found by reading both builds

The Services **panel** printed modelled **Transit and Bike** cost per acre on the
**public** build while the public Services **list** hid both services. Cause: the
panel kept its **own** per-row publish flag (`full: true`) and only the
roads+fire row ever set it, so transit and bike had nothing gating them. It
contradicted the **2026-07-28 lock** (public Services = roads supply + road cost
on both bases, *nothing else*) and the panel's **own build-aware note**.

Fixed as **its own commit `cb0dbfb`** so it is revertable alone: every row in
`SVC_COST_BASES` now names its service (`svc:`) and `svcCostRows` filters on
`SERVICES[r.svc].pub`. Public is now `lifecycle[Roads] + operating[Roads]`;
`/full/` unchanged. ⚠️ **This changed what the public site shows** — revert that
commit alone if transit/bike cost were meant to be public.

**Method note:** this was found by driving **both built builds**
(`build_site.py` → serve → read `svcCostRows(p)`), not by reading the code. The
code looked right; the `pub` tag's own comment asserts this cannot happen.

### Verify-script discipline learned here

⚠️ **The served geojson keeps `svc_cost_per_acre` until the next weekly refresh
regenerates it.** My first draft of the harness asserted the *column* was gone —
that would have been red for a week against a correct build. **Column absence is
a PIPELINE property** and is pinned in `tests/test_join_and_calculate.py`; the
verify scripts assert the **UI** only. Comments in all three scripts say so.

### Environment quirks hit this session

- ⚠️ **A backgrounded `python -m http.server` that fails to bind leaves an OLDER
  server answering, from a different directory.** Port 8791 was held by one
  started **2026-08-23** rooted at `web/`; `curl` returned 200 and I believed it.
  The tell was a **404 on a path I had just built** beside a 200 on another.
  **Test the port first (`ss -ltn`), start with `setsid … < /dev/null &`, then
  confirm `ls -la /proc/<pid>/cwd`.** New memory: `confirm-the-server-you-measure`.
- ⚠️ **`rm -rf` of a served directory leaves the server alive on a deleted cwd**,
  returning `net::ERR_EMPTY_RESPONSE`. Restart after every rebuild.
- ⚠️ **`require('playwright')` resolves from the SCRIPT's directory**, not the
  cwd. A scratch script in `/tmp` gets `MODULE_NOT_FOUND`; the same file under
  `tools/profiling/` works.
- The 42-script sweep took **~35–40 min** serially (backgrounded; it exceeded the
  600 s foreground timeout and was moved to a task file).
- `git add web/index.html` committed the panel fix together with the removal; it
  was split afterwards by `reset --soft` → revert the hunk → commit → re-apply →
  commit. Worth doing deliberately next time.
