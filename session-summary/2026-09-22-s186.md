**(b) THE BACKDROP SWEEP — this is the finding that settled the scope.** Peter
asked whether the stack forces the black backdrop. Answer: **no, and it doesn't
matter, because near-black is already the global optimum.** 19 neutral backdrops
× the real distribution (358 on-scale hoods, `current` ramp, WCAG 1.4.11 3:1,
fill vs backdrop):

| backdrop | L\* | hoods under 3:1 |
|---|---|---|
| **`#0a0a0a` (≈ today's `#0a0a0f`)** | 2.7 | **51 (14.2%)** ← best |
| `#1e1e1e` | 11.3 | 115 (32.1%) |
| `#505050` | 34.0 | 307 (85.8%) |
| `#828282` | 54.4 | 343 (95.8%) ← worst |
| `#dcdcdc` | 87.8 | 130 (36.3%) |
| `#fafafa` | 98.3 | 72 (20.1%) |

**Mechanism:** all three ramps span nearly the whole lightness axis (`current`
L\* 5.7 → 86, `glow` → 97, cividis 13 → 91). **Any backdrop whose luminance sits
inside that span collides with whatever part of the ramp matches it** — and a
mid-grey sits inside all of it. Only the extremes escape; dark wins by 6pp.
Lifting `current`'s floor clear of `#0a0a0f` needs **L\* 39 against today's 5.7**
— ~40% of its lightness range, i.e. it stops being a full-range luminance-
sequential ramp. **The black is not the problem; the ramp reaching the black is.**

This is the same geometry the codebase already found one level up — the wireframe
comment in `web/index.html`: *"There is no single colour that clears all four
ramps."*

## 3. Technical Learnings

- ⚠️ **A full-lightness-range sequential ramp cannot clear 3:1 against ANY single
  flat backdrop.** Some part of the ramp always matches the backdrop's luminance.
  This is a property of the ramp family, not of the colour black, and it is why
  "just lighten the background" has no answer. The **only** levers are compress
  the ramp's lightness range (costly — L\* 39 vs 5.7 here) or carry the low end on
  a non-fill channel (this project already does: roof edge `[40,95,120]` at L\*
  37.8, plus height).
- ⚠️ **Classifying contrast failures by "darker/lighter than the backdrop" is not
  the same as "low/high ramp value."** My sweep's column labels say low-end/high-end
  but split on luminance vs the backdrop — against a near-black backdrop *every*
  fill is lighter, so the "high-end" column there is really the low-**t** prisms.
  **The totals are right; the labels mislead.** Cross-checked against the
  independent first script (14.5% vs 14.2%) — that agreement is what caught it.
- **`check-where-the-value-can-be-wrong`, instance 11, found in a comment rather
  than a test:** `SET_ASIDE_COLOR`'s comment proves distinctness against the
  ramps' *low end* — the region where a mid-grey obviously cannot collide. The
  collision is at the mid. An assertion sited where the value can't be wrong
  passes under the bug even when it's prose.
- **A well-sourced report can still be wrong about YOUR data.** Every citation
  here checked out; the two failures were both *application* errors (wrong tail,
  wrong polarity assumption). Verifying citations is necessary and **not
  sufficient** — re-measure the claim against the actual constants.

  and dumps 1.2 MB. Name the files.
