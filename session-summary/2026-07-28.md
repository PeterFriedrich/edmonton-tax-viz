## S75 — 3. Technical Learnings

**⚠️ THE LESSON OF THE SESSION — A COMPLETENESS CHECK WHOSE ONLY EVIDENCE IS THE
DATASET UNDER TEST CAN PROVE PRESENCE, NEVER ABSENCE.** I shipped the wrong
detector first. *"Present in year N−1 AND N+1, absent from N — buildings do not
blink"* is sound, needs no external source, and catches since-demolished
properties — **but it requires the account to come back**, so it is structurally
blind to a dropout that never returns, which is exactly this defect. It reported
**5** defects for 2024 where the truth is **2,321**, and I would have read the
first map as "only 2025 is broken." Fix: a second detector against the
**independent** current roll (present in N−1 ∧ `q7d6-ambg`, absent from N), which
catches persistent dropouts and works for **every** year. Both blind spots are
documented at the top of the tool. Recorded in `DECISIONS.md` 2026-07-28.

**⚠️ TWO AGGREGATE READINGS WERE BOTH CONFIDENTLY WRONG BEFORE THE ENTITY-LEVEL
JOIN.** A `mill_class_1` split said *"80% genuine revaluation"*; a
value-of-vanished-accounts sum said *"98% vanishing"*. Both coherent, both wrong.
Only tracing individual **account numbers** and checking a **second source for
the same period** got there. **When a series has a cliff, join at the entity
level and check a second source before interpreting the shape.**

**⚠️ THE SCOPE CHECK CHANGED THE NUMBER — never file an inferred figure.** I had
"~8,000 accounts missing citywide" in `DATA.md`, the notebook and TODO. It was
**inferred from row counts of different vintages and was wrong**: most of that
gap is new construction. Measured account-by-account, the 7,929 net gap is
**8,171 likely-new** + **~2,690 in-historical-not-current (demolitions)** +
**2,448 genuine defect**. Report 2,448, never 8,000.

**⚠️ PER-YEAR DEFECT FIGURES ARE INCREMENTAL, NOT CUMULATIVE.** Each year tests
against N−1, so an account already missing in 2024 cannot be flagged again in
2025 — which is why 2025 reads 131 while ~2,448 are cumulatively absent.

**INFLATION IS THE SECOND-ORDER PROBLEM (Peter asked directly).** **The mill rate
is a residual** — council sets a budget, rate = levy ÷ total base — so a citywide
revaluation is **fiscally neutral** and a hood's burden moves only when its
assessment diverges from the city average. **CPI-deflating does not separate
those** (purchasing-power question, not tax-share). Normalize as **share of the
citywide base**: unit-free, no deflator, no vintage. Constant dollars are
reserved for **raw $/acre levels across years** only. Traps: assessed values
